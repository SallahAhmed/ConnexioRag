Project risk management is the systematic process of identifying, analyzing, and responding to potential events that could positively or negatively impact project objectives. Effective risk management is proactive rather than reactive, meaning risks are identified and addressed before they become problems. The risk management process follows a structured methodology that begins during project planning and continues throughout the project lifecycle.

The first step is risk identification where the team uses techniques like brainstorming sessions with stakeholders, checklists based on similar past projects, SWOT analysis examining strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, and expert interviews. Each identified risk is documented in a risk register with a description, category, probability, impact, and proposed response strategy. Common project risks include scope creep where requirements expand beyond the original plan, resource turnover where key team members leave, technology challenges with unfamiliar tools or frameworks, timeline pressure from unrealistic deadlines, and communication breakdowns in distributed teams.

Once risks are identified, they are analyzed for probability and impact. A probability-impact matrix categorizes risks as high, medium, or low priority. High-priority risks with both high probability and high impact require immediate attention. Risk response planning develops strategies for each significant risk. Avoidance eliminates the threat by changing the project plan. Mitigation reduces the probability or impact of the risk. Transference shifts the risk to a third party through insurance or contracts. Acceptance acknowledges the risk and budgets contingency resources to handle it if it occurs. Risks are tracked in the register and reviewed regularly in team meetings. The MasarX Monitor subgraph continuously monitors project data for early warning signs of common risks and alerts the team when risks may be materializing.
