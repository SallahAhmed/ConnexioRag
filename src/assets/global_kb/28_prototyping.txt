Prototyping is a crucial phase in the design and development process where early models of a product are created to test concepts, validate assumptions, and gather feedback before committing to full development. Prototyping saves time and resources by identifying usability issues and design flaws early when they are cheap to fix rather than during development when changes are expensive. The fidelity of a prototype should match the stage of the project.

Low-fidelity prototypes are quick and inexpensive to create, often using paper sketches, whiteboard drawings, or basic wireframing tools. They are ideal for exploring layout concepts, information architecture, and user flow without getting bogged down in visual details. Low-fidelity prototypes encourage feedback on the core functionality rather than cosmetic details. Mid-fidelity prototypes use tools like Balsamiq or Figma's basic wireframing capabilities to create more structured representations with consistent spacing, placeholder content, and basic interaction flow. High-fidelity prototypes look and function very similarly to the final product. They include realistic content, proper typography, accurate colors, and interactive transitions between screens.

Interactive prototyping tools allow designers to create clickable prototypes that simulate the real product experience. Figma, Sketch with prototyping plugins, Adobe XD, and Framer are popular tools for creating high-fidelity interactive prototypes. Prototyping connects screens with triggers like button taps, drag gestures, or timers and defines transitions with animations like slide, fade, or push effects. Usability testing with prototypes involves observing users as they attempt to complete tasks, noting where they struggle, what confuses them, and what works well. Each round of testing produces insights that inform design iterations. The goal is to validate the design before development begins, reducing the risk of expensive changes later. Even simple paper prototypes can uncover fundamental usability issues that would be costly to fix after development.
