User research is the systematic investigation of target users to understand their behaviors, needs, motivations, and pain points. It is a foundational activity in human-centered design that ensures product decisions are based on evidence rather than assumptions. User research should be conducted throughout the product lifecycle, from initial discovery to post-launch evaluation. Different research methods are appropriate for different questions and stages.

Qualitative research methods explore why and how users behave in certain ways. User interviews are one-on-one conversations that dive deep into a user's experiences, attitudes, and motivations. Contextual inquiry involves observing users in their natural environment to understand how they currently perform tasks. Focus groups bring together small groups of users to discuss their experiences and reactions to concepts. Diary studies ask users to record their experiences over time, capturing moments that might be missed in a single observation session. Usability testing observes users attempting to complete tasks with a product or prototype, identifying where they succeed and where they struggle. These methods produce rich, detailed insights but with smaller sample sizes.

Quantitative research methods measure and quantify user behavior at scale. Surveys and questionnaires collect structured data from large numbers of users, enabling statistical analysis. Analytics review examines how users interact with existing products through metrics like page views, click rates, conversion funnels, and user flows. A/B testing compares two versions of a design to determine which performs better on a specific metric. Log analysis studies server logs and clickstreams to understand user behavior patterns. These methods provide statistical validity but less contextual depth than qualitative methods. The most effective research programs combine both qualitative and quantitative methods. Research findings are typically synthesized into deliverables like user personas which are fictional characters representing user types, journey maps which visualize the user's experience over time, and affinity diagrams which organize research findings into themes.
