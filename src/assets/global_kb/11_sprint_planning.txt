Sprint planning is a collaborative event at the beginning of each sprint where the entire Scrum team comes together to define what work will be accomplished and how it will be done. The event is time-boxed to a maximum of eight hours for a one-month sprint, with proportionally less time for shorter sprints. Sprint planning answers two fundamental questions: what can be delivered in the upcoming sprint, and how will the chosen work be achieved.

The first part of sprint planning, the what, involves the Product Owner presenting the highest-priority items from the Product Backlog. The team discusses each item to ensure shared understanding of the requirements, acceptance criteria, and technical implications. The team then forecasts which items they can complete during the sprint based on their historical velocity and current capacity. This collaborative discussion often results in backlog items being split, reordered, or clarified. The outcome is a set of selected Product Backlog items that form the Sprint Backlog along with a clear Sprint Goal that provides a unifying objective for the sprint.

The second part of sprint planning, the how, involves the Development Team breaking down the selected backlog items into smaller tasks. Tasks are typically estimated in hours and represent the technical work needed to complete each item. The team discusses the technical approach, identifies dependencies, and ensures tasks are appropriately distributed. Techniques like planning poker are used for estimation where team members independently estimate effort using story points or ideal hours and discuss significant differences until consensus is reached. A well-conducted sprint planning session results in a realistic Sprint Backlog, a clear Sprint Goal that guides decision-making throughout the sprint, and a shared understanding across the entire team of what needs to be accomplished.
