Market research is the systematic process of gathering, analyzing, and interpreting information about a market, including its size, customer preferences, competitive landscape, and trends. It is a critical business activity that informs product development, marketing strategy, pricing, and expansion decisions. Companies that invest in thorough market research are significantly more likely to launch successful products and enter new markets effectively.

Primary research involves collecting original data directly from sources. Surveys and questionnaires gather structured data from large samples at relatively low cost. In-depth interviews provide rich qualitative insights into customer motivations and behaviors. Focus groups bring together small groups of target customers to discuss and react to concepts or prototypes. Observational research watches how customers behave in real purchasing or usage situations. A/B testing experimentally compares different approaches with real customers. Each method has strengths and the best research programs combine multiple methods for comprehensive understanding.

Secondary research analyzes existing data that has already been collected by others. Industry reports from firms like Gartner, Forrester, and IDC provide market sizing and forecasts. Government publications like census data and economic indicators offer demographic and economic information. Academic research provides deep analysis of specific topics. Competitor analysis examines competitors products, pricing, marketing, and customer reviews. Social media monitoring tracks online conversations about brands, products, and trends. Internal data analysis examines the company's own sales data, customer feedback, and analytics.

Key analytical frameworks help structure market research findings. SWOT analysis evaluates strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. PESTLE analysis examines political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental factors. Porter Five Forces analyzes industry competitiveness through threat of new entrants, bargaining power of buyers and suppliers, threat of substitutes, and competitive rivalry. The analysis should answer who the target customers are, what they need and value, how they make purchasing decisions, what alternatives exist, and what the market size and growth potential are.
