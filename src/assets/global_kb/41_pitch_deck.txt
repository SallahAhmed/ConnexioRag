A pitch deck is a brief presentation used to communicate a business idea to potential investors, partners, customers, or other stakeholders. It is typically delivered in person or via video call and should be concise enough to present in 5-10 minutes while generating interest and questions. A well-crafted pitch deck tells a compelling story about the problem, solution, market opportunity, and why this team is uniquely positioned to succeed.

A standard pitch deck structure includes 10-15 slides covering the following. Title slide with company name, tagline, and presenter information. Problem slide describing the specific problem being solved, ideally with a compelling story or data point that makes the problem feel real and urgent. Solution slide showing the product or service and how it solves the problem, often with a demo video or screenshots. Market size slide showing the total addressable market, serviceable addressable market, and serviceable obtainable market with credible sources. Business model slide explaining how the company makes money including pricing, customer acquisition channels, and unit economics. Competition slide showing a competitive landscape and the startup's unique advantages. Product roadmap slide showing where the product is today and where it is going. Traction slide showing key metrics like revenue, users, growth rate, partnerships, or other validation. Team slide introducing key team members with relevant experience and achievements. Financial projections slide showing revenue, expenses, and key assumptions. Funding ask slide stating how much is being raised and how the funds will be used.

Key principles for an effective pitch deck include telling a story rather than just listing facts, using visuals like images, charts, and diagrams rather than text-heavy slides, keeping slides simple with minimal text, knowing the audience and tailoring the message accordingly, being prepared to adjust the depth based on available time, practicing until the presentation flows naturally, anticipating likely questions and preparing answers, having a clear call to action at the end, and following up promptly after the pitch with additional information requested. Common pitch deck mistakes include too much text, unclear value proposition, unrealistic financial projections, not demonstrating traction, having a weak or incomplete team, and failing to clearly explain why now is the right time for this business.
