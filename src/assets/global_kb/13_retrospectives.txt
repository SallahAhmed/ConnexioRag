Sprint retrospectives are regular meetings held at the end of each sprint where the team reflects on their process and identifies actionable improvements. The retrospective is the most important Scrum event for continuous improvement because it creates a structured feedback loop that allows teams to adapt their practices based on real experience. A well-facilitated retrospective helps teams build trust, surface issues Early, and develop a culture of learning and improvement.

The classic retrospective format asks three questions: what went well, what went wrong or could be improved, and what should we do differently in the next sprint. These questions can be approached through various facilitation techniques. The Start-Stop-Continue format asks what the team should start doing, stop doing, and continue doing. The Sailboat metaphor asks what is propelling the team forward like wind, what is holding them back like an anchor, and what risks lie ahead like rocks. The 4Ls framework covers what team members Liked, Learned, Lacked, and Longed For. The Mad-Sad-Glad technique explores emotional responses to the sprint. Each format has its strengths and teams should vary their approach to keep retrospectives fresh and engaging.

A successful retrospective requires a safe environment where team members can honestly discuss challenges without fear of blame or retribution. The Scrum Master typically facilitates the retrospective, ensuring that all voices are heard and that the discussion stays constructive. The most important outcome of a retrospective is at least one concrete, actionable improvement that the team commits to implementing in the next sprint. This improvement should be specific, measurable, and assigned to a team member who will champion it. Teams that consistently act on retrospective insights show measurably better performance over time.
